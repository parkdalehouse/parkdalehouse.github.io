Money on the table

Four programs most first-time buyers underuse.

None of this is a secret, and all of it is yours if you qualify. The mistake people make is finding out about it after they have already bought.

$40K

First Home Savings Account (FHSA)

Contribute up to $8,000 a year to a lifetime maximum of $40,000. Deductible on the way in like an RRSP, tax-free on the way out like a TFSA, and never repaid. If you are renting and plan to buy within five years, this is usually the first thing to open.

$60K

Home Buyers' Plan (HBP)

Withdraw up to $60,000 from your RRSP tax-free toward a first home. You repay it to yourself over 15 years. Used alongside the FHSA, a couple can put up to $200,000 together.

$8,475

Land transfer tax rebates

Ontario refunds first-time buyers up to $4,000 of provincial land transfer tax. Buy inside Toronto and the city refunds up to a further $4,475. That is real cash at closing, and plenty of buyers never claim it properly.

30 yr

Longer amortization

First-time buyers can take a 30-year amortization on an insured mortgage instead of 25. It lowers the monthly payment and raises what you qualify for. You pay more interest over the full term, so it is a trade, and we will show you both numbers.

Questions landlords ask

Questions renters ask

Straight answers, before the call.

Can I qualify for a mortgage using rental income?

Yes. Lenders count rental income through offset or add-back formulas, and every lender applies them differently. The same file can pass at one lender and fail at another, which is exactly why the desk shops it across the panel instead of sending it to one bank.

What down payment do I need for an investment property in Ontario?

A non-owner-occupied rental typically requires 20% down. Live in one unit of a duplex or triplex and it can be less. The exact requirement depends on the lender and the property, and the desk confirms it before you start shopping.

When should I start on my mortgage renewal?

About 120 days before maturity. That is when most lenders will hold a rate, which gives the desk time to shop the file while your fallback is locked. Signing the first renewal letter without a comparison is the most expensive shortcut in this business.

What does working with a mortgage broker cost?

On most residential files the lender pays the brokerage, so there is no fee to you. Where a fee would apply, for example on some private or commercial files, it is disclosed in writing before you commit to anything.

Do you only work with PMT management clients?

No. The desk is built for landlords, and PMT owners get the file-building advantage, but any borrower in Ontario can call, including friends and family you send our way.

I rent from PMT. Does talking to you affect my tenancy?

No, and we want to be blunt about this because it matters. Your tenancy and any mortgage conversation are separate files. Nothing you tell the mortgage desk is shared with your landlord, nothing affects your lease, your rent, your renewal or your standing as a tenant, and choosing not to call changes nothing either. Your rent payment history is only ever used for a mortgage application with your written authorization, and you can withdraw it.

How much do I actually need for a down payment?

In Canada the minimum is 5% on the first $500,000 of the price, 10% on the portion between $500,000 and $1.5 million, and 20% at $1.5 million and above. So a $600,000 home needs $35,000, not $120,000. Below 20% down you pay mortgage default insurance, which gets added to the mortgage rather than paid up front.

Does my rent history actually help me qualify?

It helps, though it is not a substitute for income and credit. A documented record of on-time housing payments strengthens a file, and some lenders weigh it more heavily than others. The value of working with a broker is knowing which lenders those are instead of hoping your branch happens to be one.

Should I open an FHSA before I start looking?

Usually yes, and often earlier than people think. Contribution room starts when you open the account, not when you turn 18, so opening one even with a small deposit starts the clock. Talk to us before you move money between accounts, because the order you use the FHSA, your RRSP and your savings in changes what you owe at tax time.

Can I use the FHSA and my RRSP together?

Yes. They stack. Up to $40,000 from an FHSA plus up to $60,000 from your RRSP under the Home Buyers' Plan is up to $100,000 per person toward the same purchase, or $200,000 for a couple who both qualify. The FHSA is never repaid. The Home Buyers' Plan is repaid to your own RRSP over 15 years.

What will land transfer tax cost me?

It depends on the price and whether you buy inside Toronto, which charges its own municipal tax on top of the provincial one. First-time buyers get up to $4,000 back provincially and up to $4,475 more in Toronto. The calculator above works it out for your price, and your lawyer files the rebate at closing.

I do not think I can afford it yet. Is the call still worth it?

Especially then. Most of these calls end with a plan rather than an application: the number you are aiming at, what to fix, and how long it should take. Knowing you are eighteen months out is far more useful than guessing you are five years out.
